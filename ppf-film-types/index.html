Types of PPF Film: How to Choose the Right One for Your Car

July 17, 2025

By Fabian

If you’re thinking about protecting your car’s paint but unsure which type of Paint Protection Film (PPF) suits your needs best, you’re not alone. With various materials, finishes, and features available in Malaysia, it can get confusing fast. The good news? You’ve landed on the right page.

What is Paint Protection Film (PPF)?

Paint Protection Film (PPF) is a transparent, flexible film applied to your car’s painted surfaces to protect it from stone chips, scratches, UV damage, and chemical stains. It’s a smart investment for anyone looking to preserve their vehicle’s resale value and appearance.

Why it’s important to understand different types of PPF

Not all PPF is created equal. The differences lie in:

  • Material: TPU vs TPH vs PVC

  • Finish: Clear, Matte, Colour

  • Performance features: Self-healing, durability, warranty

Let’s explore the options.

Types of PPF by Material: TPU vs TPH vs PVC

1. Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU)

TPU PPF is considered the gold standard in the industry — and it’s what we use at P10X.

Key Benefits of TPU PPF:

  • ✅ High elasticity and flexibility for easy wrapping

  • ✅ Self-healing with heat (sunlight or engine warmth)

  • ✅ Excellent transparency for that factory finish look

  • ✅ Resistant to UV, chemicals, and road debris

  • ✅ Non-toxic and safe for paint

  • ✅ Long-lasting durability (up to 10 years)

💡 TPU is the best PPF film if you’re looking for maximum protection and long-term value.

2. Thermoplastic Polyurethane Hybrid (TPH)

TPH is a hybrid mix — cheaper than TPU, but with compromises.

Drawbacks of TPH PPF:

  • ❌ Limited self-healing ability (only works slightly)

  • ❌ Shorter lifespan (typically 2–3 years)

  • ❌ Prone to heat-related shrinking or yellowing

  • ❌ Often has a shorter or no warranty

TPH might appeal to those on a tight budget, but it’s not ideal for premium car care or long-term use.

3. Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC)

PVC PPF is an older-generation film — now largely outdated.

Why PVC PPF isn’t recommended:

  • ❌ Stiff and hard to install on curved surfaces

  • ❌ No self-healing capabilities

  • ❌ Yellowing, cracking, and peeling over time

  • ❌ Not safe for OEM paint due to potential toxicity

Modern PPF technology has moved past PVC — and so should you.

Types of PPF by Surface Finish

Clear PPF

Clear PPF is your invisible bodyguard.

  • Surface Look: Glossy and transparent, blends with original paint.

  • Ideal for: Car owners who want paint protection without changing the car’s appearance.

  • P10X Clear PPF Highlights:

    • Optical clarity

    • Enhances depth and shine

    • Almost invisible once installed

Clear PPF

Matte PPF

Want that frozen or stealth look? Matte PPF transforms your car instantly.

  • Surface Look: Satin or matte finish that diffuses reflections.

  • Cost: Slightly higher than clear PPF due to unique materials.

  • Ideal for: Car owners wanting a unique aesthetic while protecting paint.

  • P10X Matte PPF Highlights:

    • Turns glossy paint into matte

    • Premium frozen-look

    • TPU-based with full protection

Matte PPF

Colour PPF

Colour PPF offers the best of both worlds: a full body wrap with paint protection.

  • Surface Look: Gloss, matte, or satin colour finishes.

  • Cost: Higher due to pigment and TPU formulation.

  • Ideal for: Drivers who want to change their car’s look while protecting it.

  • P10X Colour PPF Highlights:

    • Made from TPU (not PVC)

    • Available in bold, elegant, or limited-edition tones

    • Scratch, chip, and fade resistant

How to Choose the Right PPF for Your Car

1. Choose Based on Surface Finish Preference

Do you want to keep your original look (clear), go bold (colour), or transform to stealth (matte)?

2. Prioritize Material Quality: TPU Over TPH

TPU gives you better protection, clarity, and lifespan.

3. Assess Key Performance Features

Self-healing, anti-yellowing, stain resistance, and UV protection.

4. Consider Durability, Lifespan, and Warranty

Think long-term. TPU can last 7–10 years with warranty support.

5. Choose the Right Coverage Package

  • Full Front

  • Full Car

  • Hybrid (front wrap + coating body)

  • Custom areas (pillars, door cups, etc.)


6. Ensure High Quality Installation

Even the best film won’t work if installed poorly. Always choose certified PPF installers. A poor job affects clarity and longevity — see why PPF is worth it in our Is PPF Worth article.

7. Evaluate Your Car’s Current Condition

New cars = best time to install. Used cars = may need paint correction first.

FAQ

Which type of PPF is the best?

TPU PPF is considered the best due to its durability, self-healing properties, and clear appearance.

Are all PPF films self-healing?

No. Only TPU-based films are truly self-healing. TPH offers limited protection and PVC has none.

How do I know if a PPF brand is using genuine TPU material?

Look for transparency, flexibility, and brand certification. Ask your installer for material data sheets.

Is PPF suitable for cars with custom or non-factory paint jobs?

Yes, but only after the paint has fully cured. Talk to a certified installer for assessment.

How long does PPF installation typically take?

1–3 days depending on coverage. Full car wraps may take longer with detailing prep included.
