Latest JPJ Tinted Rules and Specifications in Malaysia: 2025 Guide

13 Oct, 2025

By Fabian

If you’re looking into car tint Malaysia solutions in 2025, one thing is clear — you can’t ignore the JPJ tinted rules. What Are JPJ Rules on Tinted Windows in Malaysia?

The Jabatan Pengangkutan Jalan (JPJ) regulates car tint to ensure all vehicles on the road are safe — especially in low-light or rainy conditions.

Approved VLT Percentages by JPJ (2025)

Exceptions for Special Vehicles

Some vehicles are allowed a darker tint under special circumstances:

  • Medical reasons (e.g. light sensitivity, skin conditions)

  • Government, VIP, or executive vehicles with approved permits

Penalties for Non-Compliance

If your car tint does not follow JPJ specs:

  • Fine of up to RM2,000

  • Up to 6 months jail

  • Immediate removal of tint on the spot

How Car Tint Percentages Affect Visibility and What is Visible Light Transmission (VLT)?

What is VLT?

VLT (Visible Light Transmission) measures how much visible light can pass through a window. It’s expressed in percentages:

  • Higher VLT = lighter tint = more visibility

  • Lower VLT = darker tint = less visibility

Common VLT Percentages Explained

  • 70% VLT — Required for windscreen. Very light but still blocks UV.

  • 50% VLT — Legal for front side windows. Offers moderate privacy and glare reduction.

  • 35% VLT — Popular for rear windows. Darker, more private.

  • 5% VLT — “Limo tint”. Too dark for legal use without a special permit.

Why This Matters

  • Low VLT can fail Puspakom inspections

  • Dark tints can be unsafe at night or during rain

  • VLT does not equal heat rejection — even a high-VLT ceramic tint can reject heat effectively

How to Ensure Your Car Tint Passes Puspakom Inspection and JPJ Compliance?

When going for Puspakom inspection (e.g. ownership transfer, e-hailing, road tax renewal), VLT is checked using a tint meter.

Puspakom Checks for:

  • Whether the tint film meets JPJ’s minimum VLT

  • If tint is peeling, bubbling, or too dark

  • Whether the car has proper side mirrors for darker rear tints

What Happens If You Fail?

  • You’ll be asked to remove the tint

  • You must re-inspect (with added cost + hassle)

  • It can delay your road tax renewal or vehicle sale

How to Prepare

  • Use a tint specialist (like P10X) that measures your VLT before tinting

  • Don’t rely on guesswork — factory tints or DIY tints may not be compliant

  • Choose JPJ-compliant brands like 3M, Raytech, RhinePro, LLumar, and V-Kool

Common Tinting Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Before you get your car windows tinted, it’s worth learning from the common mistakes many Malaysian car owners make. From choosing the wrong VLT percentage to using cheap, non-compliant brands, these small errors can lead to inspection failures, fines, or wasted money. The good news? They’re all easily avoidable when you know what to look out for — and that’s exactly what we’ll cover below.

Choosing the Wrong VLT

Many owners mistakenly install 20% or 5% tint on front windows — which is illegal. Stick to 50% for side windows and 70% for windscreens.

Using Non-Compliant Tint Brands

Cheap tints often:

  • Fade, peel, or bubble

  • Have incorrect or unverified VLT values

  • Don’t block UV or IR rays effectively

Trusted Brands Recommended by P10X:

  • 3M Tint Malaysia (Crystalline, Ceramic IR)

  • Quad Film

  • RhinePro Tint 


Skipping Professional Installation

Even the best tint film can look bad and fail inspection if it’s poorly installed. Trust certified installers for clean lines, bubble-free results, and guaranteed compliance.

Not Applying for Special Permits

Some drivers qualify for darker tint due to medical conditions — but you need a JPJ permit. Don’t assume you’re exempt without approval.

FAQ

Can I install the darkest possible tint on my car?

Only on the rear side windows and rear windscreen — and only if your car has two functioning side mirrors. Front glass must comply with JPJ VLT rules.

Does window tint affect the resale value of my car in Malaysia?

Yes. JPJ-compliant, branded tint improves resale value. Illegal or poor-quality tint may deter buyers.

How can I check if my current car tint complies with JPJ regulations?

Use a VLT meter or visit a tint shop like P10X for a free compliance check.

What is the difference between IRR tint and ceramic tint?

IRR measures how much heat the tint blocks. Ceramic tint is the material — it blocks heat without affecting visibility or signal.

Does car tinting affect GPS, RFID, or SmartTAG?

Yes — metal-based films can interfere. We recommend ceramic or nano-ceramic films for signal-safe performance.
