5 Different Types of Car Tints: Detailed Comparison

25 Oct, 2025

By Fabian

Looking into the types of car tint for your ride in Malaysia? Excellent choice. A good car window tint film isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about comfort, privacy, UV protection, and even resale value. Whether you’re still learning the basics of window tinting, figuring out how to choose the right car tint, or weighing the benefits of window tint, this guide will walk you through the five major types of car tint—Dyed, Metalized, Carbon, Ceramic, and Hybrid. And of course, we’ll keep in mind the all-important JPJ tinted rules and specifications for Malaysian drivers in 2025.

Comparing 5 Window Tint Types: Dyed, Metalized & More

Here’s a quick comparison table for reference. Read further down for deeper details on each type.

Dyed Window Tint

Dyed window film is the most basic and affordable type of car tint. It’s made by layering dye between adhesive and protective layers. The dye absorbs sunlight, reducing glare and slightly blocking heat.

How it performs in Malaysian weather: Dyed film is popular for its dark appearance and privacy, but under Malaysia’s intense heat and humidity, it tends to fade or discolor faster.

Pros

  • Budget-friendly for first-time car owners

  • Improves privacy and reduces glare

  • Non-metallic — no signal interference

Cons

  • Limited heat rejection (mainly aesthetic)

  • Fades and turns purple over time

  • Short lifespan and limited warranty

Best For: Budget-conscious drivers or temporary cars (e.g. second vehicles).

Metalised Window Tint

Metalised tint uses tiny metallic particles to reflect heat and light instead of absorbing them. It gives your car a shiny, mirror-like finish and better heat rejection than dyed film.

How it performs in Malaysian weather: Excellent for reducing heat and UV, but can cause signal interference with GPS, SmartTAG, and mobile networks.

Pros

  • Strong heat and glare rejection

  • Reflective finish enhances privacy

  • Long lifespan and durable against scratches

Cons

  • May block GPS / SmartTAG / mobile signal

  • Can look overly reflective under bright sun

  • Not ideal for modern cars with electronic systems

Best For: Older or non-tech-heavy vehicles, or drivers who prioritise heat control and privacy.

Carbon Window Tint

Carbon tint uses carbon particles instead of metal, giving deep, rich black tones without reflectivity. It provides excellent UV and IR rejection and doesn’t interfere with signals.

How it performs in Malaysian weather: Highly resistant to fading — ideal for Malaysia’s tropical climate.

Pros

  • Strong UV & IR protection (up to 70%)

  • Matte finish, stylish look

  • No signal interference

  • Long lifespan (up to 10 years)

Cons

  • Higher cost than dyed or metalised

  • Slightly less heat rejection than ceramic

  • May look too dark for some drivers

Best For: Drivers who want a premium, fade-resistant tint without breaking the bank.

Ceramic Window Tint

Ceramic tint is the top-tier choice — made with non-metallic, nano-ceramic particles that block up to 95% infrared heat and 99% UV rays. It maintains excellent visibility day and night.

How it performs in Malaysian weather: Outstanding. Ceramic tints perform best under high heat and humidity, keeping interiors cool without darkening the glass too much.

Pros

  • Superior heat rejection (best in class)

  • Crystal-clear visibility (no haze or blur)

  • No signal interference

  • Longest lifespan & premium warranty

Cons

  • Premium price tag (RM1,000–RM2,000+)

  • Professional installation required

  • Takes longer to cure after installation

Best For: High-end cars, EV owners, or anyone seeking maximum comfort and clarity.

Hybrid Window Film

Hybrid films combine two or more technologies, usually dye + metal or dye + ceramic, to balance cost, performance, and appearance.

How it performs in Malaysian weather: Hybrid films resist fading better than dyed tints while offering improved heat rejection — a solid middle ground for long-term daily use.

Pros

  • Balanced performance (price vs quality)

  • Moderate heat & UV rejection

  • Stylish finish

  • Minimal signal issues (for ceramic hybrids)

Cons

  • Not as effective as full ceramic

  • Quality depends on brand and layer composition

  • Mid-range durability

Best For: Everyday drivers looking for value + performance without going full premium.

What Other Tinting Options Do You Have?

If you’ve heard about 3M Crystalline™ or Quad Film’s Sputtered Series, those belong to a new generation of tints known as advanced or crystalline films.

These films use multi-layer nanotechnology — capable of blocking up to 97% infrared heat even in clear, transparent shades. They’re perfect for drivers who want visibility without sacrificing heat rejection.

Other specialty tints in Malaysia include:

  • Sputtered films: multi-metal layers for high reflectivity and durability.

  • Architectural films: for sunroofs or building glass panels.

  • Sunroof PPF: for Panoramic and Sunroof for Glass protection and Cool car interior.

Which Window Car Tint Is Best for You?

Your best tint depends on your lifestyle, vehicle, and driving habits:

Law & Regulation in Malaysia

Before choosing, always check JPJ’s VLT (Visible Light Transmission) rules:

  • Front windscreen: ≥70% VLT

  • Front side windows: ≥50% VLT

  • Rear side & rear screen: ≥30% VLT

Reflective or mirrored films are not allowed without JPJ approval. You can apply for darker tints (medical or security reasons) through JPJ Borang CG1.

Maintenance & Care Tips

  • Use non-ammonia glass cleaners and a soft microfiber cloth.

  • Avoid rolling down windows for 3–5 days after installation.

  • Park in shade to prolong film life.

  • Check for bubbles or peeling every few months.

10 Major Benefits of Window Car Tint Film

Thinking window tint is just for looks? Think again. Modern car window tint films offer way more than just style—they deliver serious functional benefits, especially for drivers in Malaysia’s hot and humid climate. From heat rejection to UV protection and even enhanced safety, here are 10 key reasons why tinting your car windows is one of the smartest upgrades you can make.

  1. Heat Rejection – Keeps your cabin cool even in 35°C heat.

  2. UV Protection – Blocks up to 99% UV rays that damage skin and interior.

  3. Interior Protection – Prevents fading of leather, dashboard, and trim.

  4. Energy Efficiency – Reduces air-cond load, saving fuel or battery.

  5. Glare Reduction – Improves visibility, especially during dawn/dusk.

  6. Privacy & Security – Keeps prying eyes away and deters break-ins.

  7. Safety & Shatter Resistance – Film holds glass together in an accident.

  8. Aesthetic Appeal – Enhances your car’s style and finish.

  9. Resale Value – Maintains your car’s interior and exterior condition.

  10. Long-Term Savings – Durable tint reduces maintenance and cooling costs.

FAQ

What type of car tint is best?

It depends. For top performance choose Ceramic; for budget privacy go Dyed; for balanced value choose Hybrid or Carbon

Which tint lasts longer?

Ceramic and Carbon tend to last longest; dyed tends to fade quicker.

What is the clearest tint?

Ceramic coatings can offer high clarity with lighter tint while still offering great protection.

Which tint is best for night driving?

One with good clarity and adequate VLT; avoid ultra-dark films.

What is the best tint for a daily driver?

A mid‑budget film like Carbon or Hybrid that offers comfort, UV/heat protection and minimal compromise.
